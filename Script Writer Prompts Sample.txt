GLOBAL STYLE:
The script should always sound like a real radio host speaking live — not like an essay, not like a poem, and not like a scripted performance.

============================================================================
OPENING:
Write an opening script for a music radio show.

STRUCTURE:
1. Start with a casual greeting that welcomes both returning and first-time listeners.
2. Briefly introduce the show by describing the playlist (mention genres, variety, and overall vibe).
3. Include a line that connects with different listener motivations (e.g. music, mood, companionship).
4. End with a short, energetic transition into the show (e.g. “Let’s go”, “Let’s get into it”).

TONE & STYLE:
- Conversational and natural (like a real human speaking, not writing)
- Upbeat, positive, and confident
- Relaxed and friendly, not formal
- Inclusive and welcoming

LANGUAGE GUIDELINES:
- Use short to medium-length sentences
- Use spoken English, not literary or poetic language
- Avoid abstract or overly emotional expressions
- Use casual phrases (e.g. “a mix of…”, “we’ve got you”, “let’s go”)
- You may use dashes (—) to create natural pauses

AVOID:
- Overly poetic or reflective tone
- Long monologues or deep emotional buildup
- Complex vocabulary or philosophical statements

GOAL:
Make the listener feel immediately welcomed, lightly energized, and ready to start listening to music.

**********************************************************
EXAMPLE:
Hey, welcome back — or welcome for the first time.

Today’s show is packed with some seriously good tracks. A mix of pop, a bit of indie, maybe a few surprises — but overall, it’s all about good energy.

So whether you’re here for the music, the mood, or just something to keep you company — we’ve got you.

Let’s go.
**********************************************************

============================================================================
CLOSING:

Write a closing script for a music radio show.

STRUCTURE:
1. Start with a short and clear ending signal (e.g. “That’s a wrap”, “That’s it for today”).
2. Include a line that carries the show’s energy or mood forward (e.g. encouraging the listener to take the feeling with them).
3. End with a casual thank-you and goodbye.

TONE & STYLE:
- Light, positive, and effortless
- Friendly and human (like talking to a listener directly)
- Calm but slightly uplifting

LANGUAGE GUIDELINES:
- Keep sentences short and clean
- Use simple, everyday vocabulary
- Use casual expressions (e.g. “thanks for hanging out”, “see you soon”)
- Focus on clarity and flow, not depth

AVOID:
- Long summaries of the show
- Moral lessons or “big conclusions”
- Emotional or poetic overstatement
- Formal or scripted-sounding language

GOAL:
End the show smoothly, leave the listener with a positive feeling, and maintain a sense of easy companionship.

**********************************************************
EXAMPLE:
And that’s a wrap.

Take that energy with you — wherever you’re headed next.

Thanks for hanging out, and I’ll see you soon.
**********************************************************

TRANSITION:
Write a natural transition script for a music radio show between two songs where you think is appropriate.


CORE TASK:
Create a smooth, engaging transition that connects the two songs. The transition must reflect the relationship between them (e.g. energy shift, emotional contrast, stylistic similarity, or artist context).

TONE & STYLE:
- Conversational and natural (like a real radio host speaking live)
- Smooth, relaxed, and human
- Lightly engaging, not overly dramatic
- Confident but not scripted
- Suitable for a modern pop / R&B / playlist-style show

LANGUAGE GUIDELINES:
- Use short to medium-length sentences
- Use spoken English (not poetic or literary)
- Avoid abstract or vague statements
- Be specific to the songs/artists when possible
- It should sound like something actually said on air

---

TECHNIQUE USAGE:

Each transition should combine:
- 2–4 FUNCTION techniques (what you say)
- 1–2 EXPRESSION techniques (how you say it)

Do NOT overload. Keep it natural.

---

FUNCTION TECHNIQUES (choose naturally):

1. Song Reflection — briefly describe the previous song’s vibe or impact  
2. Song Intro — introduce the next song naturally  
3. Theme Talk — connect to the show’s theme or emotional direction  
4. Companion Talk — speak directly to the listener (“you”)  
5. Music Context — add relevant artist/song info (collab, era, style)  
6. Storytelling — suggest a simple scene or relatable moment  
7. Branding — lightly reference the show or host  
8. Energy Shift — highlight how the mood is changing or continuing  

---

EXPRESSION TECHNIQUES (enhance naturally):

1. Sensory Language — make the music feel physical or emotional (“feel that”, “hits”, “wraps around you”)  
2. Physical Engagement — invite listener movement (“nod your head”, “let it move you”)  
3. Emotional Carryover — extend the feeling forward (“take that energy with you”)  
4. Tone Control — keep it light, medium, or slightly expressive (avoid heavy/poetic tone)

---

OUTPUT:
- Write 2–3 variations
- Each variation should feel slightly different
- Keep each transition concise (2–5 lines max)
